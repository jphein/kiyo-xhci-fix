Subject: [PATCH v9 0/2] media: uvcvideo: Quirks for Razer Kiyo Pro firmware lockup

The Razer Kiyo Pro (1532:0e05) firmware locks up in a way that cascades to
xhci_hc_died(), disconnecting every device on the bus -- keyboard and mouse
included -- requiring a hard reboot. USB_QUIRK_NO_LPM (patch 1 of the original
3-patch series) is merged and backported to stable 6.1/6.6/6.12/6.18/6.19; it
removes one trigger (LPM-transition reinit failure). This series adds the
remaining uvcvideo device quirks.

What changed since v8 -- and it is a real change of understanding, not a tweak.
I now have usbmon captured through real crashes, and I had the mechanism wrong.

  The v8 series claimed UVC_QUIRK_CTRL_THROTTLE prevents the lockup by rate-
  limiting control transfers, with a COMMIT-specific delay and an extended
  COMMIT URB timeout. Wire data from two real crashes (2026-06-13, v8.1 loaded)
  plus a re-analysis of the 2026-05-30 capture with the isochronous plane
  included shows that is not what happens. I am withdrawing those claims.

The actual failure is a two-phase stream-reconfiguration lock (identical on the
heavy-churn 05-30 WebRTC call and the single-reconfiguration 06-13 call):

  Phase 1: SET_INTERFACE alt 0 (stop stream) is submitted and HANGS ~5.4s. The
           isochronous video endpoint keeps delivering frames normally through
           the entire hang (5000+ frames, ~2ms jitter). The control endpoint is
           unresponsive while data flows -- a control-plane stall. The driver
           eventually unlinks the request (-2/ENOENT).
  Phase 2: ~2ms later the COMMIT_CONTROL SET_CUR is submitted. ~26ms after that
           the iso endpoint wedges (goes silent for the duration of the hang),
           and the COMMIT itself hangs ~5-7s, completing -108 (ETIMEDOUT) if the
           control timeout reaps it, or -32 (EPIPE) if a port-cycle reaps it
           first. The submit->iso-wedge offset is a consistent 25-28ms across
           every event in both captures.

Two consequences for the patch series:

1. The trigger is a single reconfiguration (one SET_INTERFACE + one COMMIT), not
   a rapid SET_CUR burst. A minimum-interval throttle has nothing to pace and
   cannot prevent this. The COMMIT-specific 200ms delay and the 10s COMMIT URB
   timeout from v8 are dropped: the wire shows the hung COMMIT simply hangs
   longer with the extended timeout (it sat the full 10s in one capture), so the
   timeout does not aid recovery, and the 200ms delay targets a COMMIT-rate
   effect that does not exist.

2. The descriptor bug is not the real-crash mechanism. The SuperSpeed Endpoint
   Companion descriptor for EP5 IN (interrupt) declares wBytesPerInterval=8 vs
   wMaxPacketSize=64, and the concern was that xHCI underallocation produces a
   COMP_SHORT_PACKET flood. In the real crash EP5 is silent: 89 events total
   across a 437s call (all teardown artifacts, zero short-packet completions),
   versus ~994,000 in the synthetic interrupt-hammer. The high-volume traffic is
   the healthy iso video stream. So the COMP_SHORT_PACKET-flood path does not
   fire in a real crash; the failure is an iso/control firmware lock independent
   of the EP5 descriptor bug. (This narrows what an xHCI-side fix needs to
   target -- see below.)

Scope of this series, stated honestly: it does NOT fix the cascade. The cascade
is a host-controller-level failure -- the controller dies when an endpoint
wedges -- and belongs to the xHCI layer (Mathias Nyman / Michal Pecio). What
this series does:

  Patch 1/2: UVC_QUIRK_CTRL_THROTTLE, a single uniform minimum interval between
    control transfers. It does not prevent the lockup. The honest claim is
    narrower: by spacing control traffic it can reduce how often the firmware is
    driven into the lockup. [PENDING: this frequency-reduction claim is not yet
    isolated from the NO_LPM and workload confounds; a stock-kernel A/B is
    planned. If you send before that result, state the claim as conjecture.]

  Patch 2/2: Razer Kiyo Pro device-table entry with UVC_QUIRK_CTRL_THROTTLE,
    UVC_QUIRK_DISABLE_AUTOSUSPEND and UVC_QUIRK_NO_RESET_RESUME. The latter two
    address the autosuspend/resume reinit path (the 2026-06-06 crash was post-
    resume), in line with quirks already carried for other fragile UVC firmware
    (Insta360 Link, Logitech Rally Bar). They are mitigations, not a fix.

For the xHCI maintainers: the useful new datum is that this is reproducible
host-controller death triggered by a stream reconfiguration, with EP5 quiet and
the iso endpoint wedging ~26ms after a COMMIT_CONTROL during the reconfiguration.
Michal's COMP_SHORT_PACKET retry path (process_bulk_intr_td) does not run for
iso and would not engage here; whether the max_esit_payload clamp changes the
Phase-1 control stall or the Phase-2 iso wedge is the open question. [PENDING:
re-run on 6.17.0-xhci-test with usbmon is planned to answer this directly.]

Why kernel-side is the only path: the firmware bug cannot be fixed on shipped
devices. The wBytesPerInterval byte is at offset 0x1F570A in the image shipped
with Razer's Windows updater; we reverse-engineered the UVC Extension Unit flash
protocol (XU6 sel 3, 32-byte chunks, status 0x82 = success) and implemented a
Linux flasher. The device acknowledges writes and reports burn-complete but does
not persist across a power cycle, and the ROM-boot recovery path is locked out
in production firmware. Forensics in firmware-analysis/ in the linked repo.

Test data (current understanding):
  - Synthetic rapid-SET_CUR flood (stress-test-kiyo.sh): stock crashes at round
    ~25; with a uniform throttle active, 500+ rounds clean. This confirms the
    throttle handles the rapid-burst path -- but the rapid burst is NOT the
    real-world failure (see wire analysis above), so this is necessary-not-
    sufficient and should not be read as "the throttle fixes the crash."
  - Real-world: 4 hc_died in 41 min on a 50ms uniform throttle (05-13); far rarer
    since (2 watchdog-caught precursors across many hours on the layered v8.1),
    but the captures show the surviving failures are the reconfiguration lock the
    throttle cannot prevent.
  - Operational pattern consistent with accumulated device state: every session
    begun on a freshly enumerated camera has run clean (4/4 -- 61-min WebRTC,
    2-h OBS continuous capture, two further calls), while sessions begun on an
    enumeration hours-to-days old account for 4 of 5 failures captured. The
    exceptions cut both ways: one 72-min call on a 25-hour-old enumeration ran
    clean (2026-07-09), and one precursor fired after 70+ min of continuous
    streaming on a freshly cycled camera (2026-06-13, caught by a userspace
    watchdog before hc_died). Small n, uncontrolled workloads -- suggestive
    only -- but re-enumerating the camera before use is the most effective
    operational mitigation found so far, which is why we read the lockup as
    accumulated firmware state rather than a race a throttle could close.
  - Wire captures (the load-bearing evidence): crash-evidence/2026-06-13-
    precursor-wire-captures/ (RESULTS.md + usbmon) and the 05-30 cross-capture
    comparison therein. Hammerint and stream-mmap-loop results on Intel Cannon
    Lake unchanged from v8 (both clean; EP5 cancel-hammer and pure streaming do
    not reproduce hc_died on Intel).

Tested on: 6.17.0-35-generic (Ubuntu HWE), 6.17.0-xhci-test (Michal's
max_esit_payload + COMP_SHORT_PACKET retry), 6.8.0-106-generic. Intel Cannon
Lake PCH xHCI (8086:a36d). Two Razer Kiyo Pro units, fw 1.5.0.1 (bcdDevice 8.21).
